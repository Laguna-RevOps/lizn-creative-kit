The Brief, Decoded
The creative challenge
isn't awareness.
It's identity.

LIZN makes hearpieces, not hearing aids. That distinction is everything. This is a product built for a consumer who doesn't see themselves in any current category: not the OTC hearing aid buyer, not the Apple AirPods user, not the aging boomer reaching for a medical device.

LIZN's customer is a 40–60-year-old who grew up in the crowd at concerts, still goes to loud restaurants and industry events, and has started noticing, with a mix of frustration and quiet denial, that they're asking people to repeat themselves more often.

The Real Problem
They don't identify with "hearing loss"
They identify with being sharp, social, present, and engaged. Our creative has to meet them where they are, not where their audiologist is.
The Secondary Problem
Visuals produced abroad feel foreign
LIZN's existing imagery doesn't feel US-native, contemporary, or relatable to this specific American consumer. We solve this with a stock and AI-first approach that prioritizes authenticity over polish.
The Opportunity
Performance tech framing, not medical
Position LIZN the way Whoop, OURA, or high-end earbuds are positioned: as an intentional upgrade, not a concession to aging.
The Constraint
No US shoot budget. Stock + AI approach.
Optimized stock (Unsplash, Getty) + AI generation for product context shots. Two A/B-testable campaign kits with 4 static ads each.

Audience Definition
Two personas.
One product.
Primary Persona | Kit A
"The Convener"
Ages 42–62. Professionally accomplished. Socially active. Goes to dinner parties, client dinners, live events, open-plan offices.

Ages 38–50. More tech-forward. Already uses AirPods, a Whoop, maybe a continuous glucose monitor. Sees LIZN as a performance upgrade, not a concession.

Will evangelize to their social circle once they discover it.

Pain Map & Desire Map
Where the audience
actually lives.
Pain Map
What they're experiencing
Surface pain: "I keep asking people to repeat themselves." The repeated lean-in. The excuse about the restaurant being loud.
Real pain: Social anxiety. Slight embarrassment. The micro-adjustments nobody notices but the person making them, like pre-positioning near the speaker and nodding when they haven't heard.
Denied pain: "My hearing is fine, it's just loud in here." The rationalization. This is exactly where LIZN lives because it validates the rationalization rather than challenging it.
Identity threat: Anything that signals "I'm losing something" is a non-starter. Hearing aids = old. LIZN must feel like an upgrade.
Desire Map
What they actually want
To be the sharpest person in the room, not the one who's half-there asking for repeats.
Technology that is completely invisible: no wires, no over-ear components, nothing that broadcasts "I need help hearing."
Something that feels like an upgrade, not an accommodation, framed the way AirPods or a Whoop are framed.
To stop noticing they're wearing it because it just works: natural sound, no distortion, no over-amplification.
Relief. The quiet satisfaction of not having to work as hard just to follow a conversation.

Emotional & Logical Drivers

Emotional Drivers
Primary: Confidence. Presence.
The feeling of being effortlessly engaged in any environment. Not straining. Not leaning in. Just fully there.

Secondary: Relief. The quiet satisfaction of not having to work as hard just to follow a conversation.

Avoidance: Social embarrassment. The fear of looking old, checked-out, or cognitively off.
Logical Drivers
The rational case for LIZN
Nearly a decade of development. This isn't a gadget, it's a refined tool.

Clarifies speech; doesn't amplify noise. That's the key technological differentiator that separates it from cheap amplifiers.

Discreet: smaller than AirPods, no over-ear wire, no visible signal.

Dual-mode: also works as earphones for music and calls.

Messaging Pillars
Three pillars.
All creative flows
through one of them.
P1
Clarity, Not Volume
The technology story. LIZN doesn't just amplify. It distinguishes by lifting speech from noise, sharpening consonants, and reducing acoustic clutter. Sophisticated engineering, invisibly delivered.
P2
Stay in the Conversation
The social/emotional story. The restaurant table. The client dinner. The first date. LIZN keeps you fully present in the moments that matter, without anyone knowing you're wearing it.
P3
The Anti-Hearing Aid
The identity story. Designed for people who don't need hearing aids. Engineered for real life. For the generation that grew up loud and refuses to dial down.
Objection Handling

The four objections our creative must pre-empt.

"I don't need a hearing aid."
→LIZN isn't one. It's for everyone who has ever struggled to hear in noise, which is everyone. The category reframe is the campaign.
"It'll look obvious."
→One of the world's smallest earphones. No wire, no over-ear anything. This must be stated visually in ads. Never show the product on an ear in a clinical or obvious way.
"It probably just turns everything up."
→The exact opposite. It isolates and clarifies speech while reducing background noise. "Not loud. Just clear." is the four-word answer to this objection.
"It's probably expensive for what it does."
→Emphasize nearly a decade of development. Frame against cheap amplifiers that make everything louder. LIZN is the precision alternative, not a commodity product.

Confidential. 2025. Boombit. LIZN Project.
Cross-Campaign Primary Line
Every word.
Recommended Primary Campaign Line
Every word. Every room.

Universal without being clinical. Captures both the functional promise (comprehension) and the aspirational one (every environment, every social context). Works as an umbrella across both Kit A and Kit B, and pairs with the product's core use case in a memorable, repeatable format.

Client Starter Lines | Assessed

Line Assessment Recommended Use
"Not loud. Just clear." Strong: the product differentiator in four words. Answers the #1 objection before it's raised. Works as a supporting headline and product descriptor. Kit A, Angle 3 headline. Can also be used as a product descriptor across both kits.
"Clearly Better." Generic. Works as short-form digital hook but doesn't own a specific territory. Doesn't signal the category reframe. Short hook / CTA line only. Not a primary.
"More clear. Better hear." Rhyme is a risk. It edges toward folksy and approachable rather than premium. Works for organic/social but not for paid. Skip for paid media. Possibly usable in organic social captions.
"Proud. Not Loud." Punchy and slightly combative, memorable but the "proud" angle is emotionally ambiguous (proud of what?). Could work as a retargeting hook. Retargeting / second-touch ads. A/B test against primary line.
"A clear focus on what's ahead." Aspirational and well-written. Slightly long for ad copy but strong for landing page or hero text. Landing page hero or video ad VO.

Kit A | Visual Direction
Art Direction Principles
Visual Tone
Warm, textured confidence. Lived-in, real, American. Not aspirational luxury, but people who are actually having a great time.
Photo Style
Candid and editorial. Mid-shot and tight focus on faces and reactions. Not product-forward because the tech is invisible, which is the point.
Color Tone
Warm amber, deep wood tones, low-lit interiors. The palette should feel like a Saturday night at a good restaurant, not a hospital or tech shoot.
Environments
Upscale casual restaurant, lively bar with ambient music, WeWork-style open office, coffee shop counter conversation, outdoor event.
Stock Direction
Unsplash/Getty: "candid restaurant conversation," "business dinner laughing," "friends bar nightlife candid." Ages 40s–early 60s. US-native casting.
Avoid
Over-ear devices. AARP-adjacent stock. White backgrounds. Product-first framing. Anyone looking frustrated or struggling.
